💬 Idioms

Travel Idioms

Common English idioms about travel, packing, and trips. Each includes meaning, example, and Arabic hint.

🛣️ Hit the road

Meaning: To start a journey or leave a place

Example: "It's getting late — we should hit the road if we want to reach Hurghada before dark."

Arabic: يطلع في الطريق / يبدأ رحلته

🎒 Travel light

Meaning: To travel with very little luggage

Example: "For a weekend trip, I always travel light — just a carry-on bag."

Arabic: يسافر بأقل أمتعة ممكنة

🧳 Live out of a suitcase

Meaning: To travel so often that you never fully unpack

Example: "Her job means she lives out of a suitcase half the year."

Arabic: يعيش من شنطة سفره — دايمًا مسافر

🗺️ Off the beaten track

Meaning: A place that not many tourists visit

Example: "Siwa Oasis is off the beaten track, but it's worth the trip."

Arabic: مكان بعيد عن الزحمة السياحية

🏡 A home away from home

Meaning: A place where you feel as comfortable as at home

Example: "That small hotel in Luxor became a home away from home for us."

Arabic: بيت تاني بره بيته

🌴 Get away from it all

Meaning: To take a break from your normal, busy life

Example: "We just need a weekend in the North Coast to get away from it all."

Arabic: يهرب من ضغوط الحياة شوية

✨ Once in a lifetime

Meaning: A special experience that may never happen again

Example: "Watching the sunrise over the pyramids was a once-in-a-lifetime trip."

Arabic: تجربة عمرها ما هتتكرر

📝 Grammar

will / going to / present continuous

📊 Grammar Slides

1 / 1

⚡ will = Decided right now

Use will for a casual plan you decide at the moment of speaking — no ticket bought, no plan made yet.

SubjectVerbExample
I / You / We / They / He / She / Itwill + base verb"I forgot to call my dad! I will call him after class."
Remember: "will" never changes form. Always: will + base verb, for every subject.

📅 going to = Decided earlier (a plan)

Use going to when you decided before the moment of speaking — you already have the intention or the ticket.

Subject + begoing toExample
I amgoing to"I am going to go to a concert tomorrow." (ticket already bought)
She is / He is / It isgoing to"Ayman is going to travel next Thursday."
You are / We are / They aregoing to"Heba and her friends are going to visit the museum on Friday."

⏰ present continuous = Already arranged

Use be + verb-ing for future events that are already planned, arranged, or fixed — often with a specific time. Also used for timetables and schedules.

Subject + beVerb+ingExample
I am / you are / he-she-it is / we-they areverb+ing"I am visiting my doctor today at 5:00 pm."
Schedule/timetableverb+ing"The train is leaving at 8:00 pm."

🚨 THE BIG MISTAKE

In Arabic, we often use "ha-" or "sawfa" for almost any future action, so Arabic speakers tend to overuse "will" in English — even when a plan is already decided.

❌ "I will go to Spain next week." (if the ticket is already booked) — this sounds unnatural in English.
✅ "I am going to go to Spain next week." or "I am flying to Spain next week."

Ask yourself: did I decide this just now (will), or did I already plan it (going to / present continuous)?

📖 Reading

Preparing For A Trip

Maria is excited because she is going on a trip! First, she needs to check her documents. She takes out her passport. Then, she looks at her airline ticket and boarding pass. Maria puts these important documents in her wallet. She also checks that she has some money and her credit card.

Next, Maria starts to pack her clothes. She decides to fold her T-shirts, shorts, and dresses nicely. She also picks a jacket for the cold evenings. Maria likes to arrange her clothes in the suitcase so she can find everything easily.

Maria knows she will go to the beach, so she adds her swimsuit and sunglasses to her bag. She also remembers to pack socks, underwear, and pajamas. To keep her feet comfortable, she chooses her favorite shoes and slippers.

After finishing her clothes, Maria moves on to her electronics. She takes her mobile phone and laptop. She makes sure to include the chargers for both devices. Maria also packs her headphones so she can listen to music during the flight.

Maria then goes to the bathroom to get some personal items. She needs to pack her toothbrush, toothpaste, hair comb, and sunscreen. These are essential for her trip, especially since she plans to spend a lot of time in the sun.

Finally, Maria looks at her suitcase and checks if she has everything. She wants to make sure she has not forgotten anything. Happily, she closes her suitcase and feels ready for her lovely trip!

Q4 note: her novel is never mentioned in the passage — watch for items that are NOT there.

🎭 Roleplay

Roleplay Scenarios

🧳 Scenario 1: Packing for Hurghada

  • Mona and Ahmed are traveling to Hurghada tomorrow
  • They are packing and checking what they've already planned
  • Practice will / going to / present continuous

Mona: Hi Ahmed! Are you excited about our trip to Hurghada?

Ahmed: Yes, I am! I'm packing my bag right now. What about you?

Mona: I'm going to pack my suitcase tonight. I already have my passport and boarding pass ready.

Ahmed: Great! Don't forget your credit card.

Mona: I won't forget! I'm also folding my clothes. I'm taking T-shirts, shorts, and my swimsuit for the beach.

Ahmed: Sounds good! I'm bringing my sunglasses. It's going to be sunny all week!

Mona: Oh wait, my mom is calling. I will answer it and call you back!

📅 Scenario 2: Trip Schedule to Alexandria

  • Omar is planning a trip with Sara and their friends
  • They discuss the schedule day by day
  • Practice going to + present continuous for a fixed plan

Omar: Here's the schedule I made for Alexandria. On Friday, we're going to arrive at 10:00 am and check in to the hotel.

Sara: Great! What are we doing Friday evening?

Omar: We're having dinner at a seafood restaurant on the Corniche — I already booked a table.

Sara: Perfect. And Saturday?

Omar: On Saturday, we're going to visit the Library of Alexandria and go sightseeing around the citadel.

Sara: Sounds amazing! I'll bring my camera to take lots of photos.

Omar: Good idea! According to the schedule, our bus back to Cairo is leaving Sunday at 6:00 pm.

🏢 Scenario 3: At the Travel Agency

  • Heba is asking a travel agent about a trip to Luxor
  • Practice the difference between "will" (deciding now) and "going to" (already planned)

Heba: Hi, I'd like some information about trips to Luxor.

Agent: Of course! When are you going to travel?

Heba: I'm not sure yet — maybe next month. What do you recommend?

Agent: We have a 3-day package that includes the Valley of the Kings and a boat trip on the Nile.

Heba: That sounds lovely! OK, I will book it right now — you've convinced me!

Agent: Great choice! You're going to have an amazing time.

📋 Summary

Quick Cheat Sheet

⚡ will (Decided now)

Subject + will + base verb
"I will call him after class."
"We will go hiking."

📅 going to (Decided earlier)

Subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb
"She is going to travel next week."

⏰ present continuous (Arranged)

Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
"I am visiting my doctor at 5 pm."
"The train is leaving at 8 pm." (timetable)

🚨 Big Mistake to Avoid

Don't use "will" for plans you already made. Arabic "ha-/sawfa" pushes us to overuse "will" — ask: decided now, or already planned?
