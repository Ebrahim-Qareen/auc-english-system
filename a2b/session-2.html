💬 Idioms

Workplace Idioms

Seven idioms you can use in real conversations. Each includes meaning, example, and Arabic hint.

🏁 Call it a day

Meaning: Stop working for the day

Example: "We’ve finished the report — let’s call it a day."

Arabic: يقفل الشغل للنهارده

🕯️ Burn the midnight oil

Meaning: Work late into the night

Example: "She burned the midnight oil to finish the presentation."

Arabic: يسهر لما يخلّص شغله

🌨️ Be snowed under

Meaning: Have far too much work to do

Example: "I’m snowed under this week — can we meet on Sunday?"

Arabic: غرقان في الشغل

🎩 Wear many hats

Meaning: Do several different jobs or roles

Example: "In a small company you wear many hats — sales, support, and admin."

Arabic: بيشيل أكتر من دور

💼 Get down to business

Meaning: Start dealing with the important work

Example: "Let’s get down to business and review the schedule."

Arabic: يدخل في صلب الموضوع

👞 Put your best foot forward

Meaning: Try to make the best possible impression

Example: "It’s your first interview — put your best foot forward."

Arabic: يقدّم أحسن صورة لنفسه

📐 Back to the drawing board

Meaning: Start again because the plan did not work

Example: "The client rejected the design, so it’s back to the drawing board."

Arabic: نرجع نبدأ من أول وجديد

📝 Grammar

Gerunds & Infinitives

📊 Grammar Slides

1 / 1

🎯 Gerund = verb + ing (used like a noun)

Use the gerund in these four situations:

RuleExample
As the subject of a sentenceSmoking is bad for your health.
After a prepositionI am responsible for organizing the meetings.
After certain expressionsI look forward to hearing from you.
After certain verbsShe enjoys working with numbers.
Gerund expressions to memorize: can't stand • can't help • feel like • be used to • get used to • look forward to • there's no point in

🎯 Infinitive = to + verb

Use the infinitive in these four situations:

RuleExample
After adjectivesIt is nice to eat outside in the spring.
To express purpose or reasonMagdy is learning English to visit London.
verb + object + infinitiveHabiba asked her mother to make her a sandwich.
After certain verbsSiham plans to go to the interview tomorrow.

📋 The Verb Lists (memorize these!)

Followed by GERUNDFollowed by INFINITIVE
avoid, can't help, consider, discuss, dislike, don't mind, enjoy, imagine, involve, keep, miss, practice, suggest, understand, finish, recommendagree, appear, ask, choose, decide, expect, fail, hope, learn, manage, offer, plan, refuse, seem, wait, arrange, would like, promise, want, invite
Both forms are fine after: allow • begin • encourage • finish • forget • need • remember • start • stop • try • like • love • prefer

BaseGerundRule
makemakingdrop the final -e

🚨 THE BIG MISTAKE

After a preposition (for, at, in, about, to as a preposition), English always uses verb + ing — never "to + verb".

❌ I am responsible for organize the meetings.
❌ I look forward to hear from you.
✅ I am responsible for organizing the meetings.
✅ I look forward to hearing from you.

Watch out: "look forward to" and "be used to" — that "to" is a preposition, so a gerund follows it.

📖 Reading

A Day at the Office

Tom works at a busy office in the city. He is responsible for managing the office supplies. Every morning, he starts by checking the supply. Tom enjoys organizing the items and keeping everything in order. His coworker Anna helps him with this task. Anna has her responsibilities, too. She plans meetings and sends invitations to everyone. Anna also likes preparing for the meetings by setting up the room. Sometimes, she is responsible for arranging food and hot drinks. Together, Tom and Anna work well as a team.

Yesterday, their boss asked them to plan a big event. It was exciting but difficult. Tom and Anna knew they needed to work together. They began by choosing the location and making a list of guests. They always enjoy helping each other. Tom was in charge of creating the schedule, and he spent the afternoon preparing for any changes. Anna's job was to call the food company and make the bookings.

Working as a team, they made everything easier and more fun. In the end, they successfully managed the event. Their boss was happy and thanked them for their hard work. Tom and Anna felt proud and enjoyed being part of the team.

Office supplies = the things you use to do your work in an office (pens, paper, notebooks).

🎭 Roleplay

Roleplay Scenarios

🎤 Scenario 1: The Job Interview (3 people)

  • Two people interview one candidate
  • Agree on the job first (HR, chef, teacher…)
  • Ask about education, experience, skills, and future plans
  • The candidate must ask 1–2 questions at the end

Interviewer 1: Thank you for coming. Can you tell us about your education?

Candidate: I studied business, and I finished a course in accounting last year.

Interviewer 2: What do you enjoy doing at work?

Candidate: I enjoy solving problems and I like working with a team.

Interviewer 1: What are your future plans?

Candidate: I hope to learn more about project management. I plan to take a course next year.

Interviewer 2: Do you have any questions for us?

Candidate: Yes — how will my performance be evaluated? And what do you like most about working here?

📅 Scenario 2: Planning an Event at Work

  • You and a colleague must plan a company event
  • Divide the tasks between you
  • Use: responsible for + ing, in charge of + ing

You: The boss asked us to plan the event. Where should we start?

Colleague: I suggest choosing the location first.

You: Good idea. I’ll be in charge of creating the schedule.

Colleague: Then I’m responsible for calling the food company and making the bookings.

You: Don’t forget sending the invitations.

Colleague: I won’t. I enjoy organizing this kind of thing!

👔 Scenario 3: Describing Your Job to a New Colleague

  • A new employee asks what you do all day
  • Describe your daily routine and tasks
  • Use workplace verbs: lead, organize, check, prepare, explain

New colleague: So, what do you actually do here?

You: I lead a small team. I organize our meetings and check that everyone finishes on time.

New colleague: That sounds busy. What’s your morning like?

You: I usually start by checking emails, then I prepare the day’s tasks.

New colleague: Do you enjoy it?

You: Yes! I enjoy helping people and I don’t mind working under pressure.
