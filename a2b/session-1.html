💬 Idioms

Work & Career Idioms

Seven idioms you can use in real conversations. Each includes meaning, example, and Arabic hint.

🚪 Get your foot in the door

Meaning: Get a small first job that can lead to a better one later

Example: "I took an internship to get my foot in the door at that company."

Arabic: يبدأ بخطوة صغيرة عشان يدخل المجال

🤗 A people person

Meaning: Someone who enjoys and is good at working with others

Example: "She is a people person, so customer service suits her perfectly."

Arabic: شخص اجتماعي بيحب التعامل مع الناس

🪜 Climb the career ladder

Meaning: Move up to better and higher positions at work

Example: "He worked hard for five years to climb the career ladder."

Arabic: يترقّى ويطلع في السلّم الوظيفي

🎯 Land a job

Meaning: Successfully get a job you applied for

Example: "She landed a job at a big company after only two interviews."

Arabic: يجيب الشغل / يتقبل في الوظيفة

📖 Know something inside out

Meaning: Know a subject extremely well

Example: "Zain knows Python inside out — he can solve any problem with it."

Arabic: فاهم الحاجة كويس جدًا

💪 Have what it takes

Meaning: Have the skills and qualities needed to succeed

Example: "I think you have what it takes to be a great manager."

Arabic: عنده المؤهلات والقدرة اللازمة

🏃 Go the extra mile

Meaning: Do more than what is expected of you

Example: "Our best employees always go the extra mile for clients."

Arabic: يبذل مجهود زيادة عن المطلوب

📝 Grammar

Modals of Ability: can / can't / could / be able to

📊 Grammar Slides

1 / 1

✅ can / can't — Present Ability

Use can for what you are able to do now, and can't for what you are not able to do.

PositiveNegative
I can ride a bike.I can't ride a bike.
She can speak French.She can't speak French.
I am able to work in a team.I am not able to work late.
Form: can / could + base verb (no "to"). ✅ "Adam can sing"  ❌ "Adam can to sing"

⏪ could / couldn't — Past Ability

Use could and couldn't for what you were able (or not able) to do in the past.

couldcouldn't
She could paint before she started school.I couldn't cook until I went to university.
He could read before he turned five.I couldn't drive until I was 18.

❓ Making Questions & Short Answers

Switch the modal and the subject to make a question.

StatementQuestionShort answer
You can swim.Can you swim?Yes, I can. / No, I can't.
She is able to speak French.Is she able to speak French?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
He could ride a bike.Could he ride a bike?Yes, he could. / No, he couldn't.

🚨 THE BIG MISTAKE

"can" and "could" never change and never take "to".

❌ She cans speak English.  ❌ Can you to speak French?  ❌ He could to swim.
✅ She can speak English.  ✅ Can you speak French?  ✅ He could swim.

But "be able to" DOES take "to": "I am able to work in a team."

Zain: The Programmer

Zain is a skilled programmer who went to a well-known university. He studied Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence. He also has certificates in Web Development, Data Analysis, and Programming, and a diploma in Graphic Design.

Zain has many important job skills. He is very organized, which helps him manage his time well. Zain says, "I can make software applications that help people." He also communicates well with his co-workers, making teamwork easy, and they often say, "You are an amazing team player."

With his experience in programming, Zain can solve problems fast. He likes working with others to find solutions. Zain enjoys learning new technologies and getting better at his skills. His education and abilities make him a strong choice for many jobs. Zain's hard work and creativity will help him succeed as a programmer!

🎭 Roleplay

Roleplay Scenarios

💼 Scenario 1: Your First Job Interview

  • You are applying for a junior office job in Cairo
  • The interviewer asks about your skills and qualifications
  • Answer using can / can't / be able to

Interviewer: Good morning! Please have a seat. Can you tell me about your qualifications?

You: Good morning. I have a degree in business, and I did a course in office management last year.

Interviewer: Great. Are you able to work in a team?

You: Yes, I am. I have good teamwork and communication skills.

Interviewer: Can you use Excel?

You: Yes, I can. Two years ago I couldn't use it at all, but I practiced a lot and now I can build reports.

Interviewer: Excellent. You're hired!

📄 Scenario 2: Helping a Friend With a CV

  • Your friend Mona is updating her CV
  • Ask about her education, experience, and references
  • Give advice about what to add

You: I heard you are looking for a job. How is it going?

Mona: I'm updating my CV with my skills and qualifications.

You: What are your strongest skills?

Mona: Teamwork and problem-solving. I can find solutions quickly.

You: Do you have any work experience?

Mona: Yes, two years of experience in marketing.

You: Did you ask anyone for a reference?

Mona: Yes, my old manager will be my reference. Good luck to me!

💭 Scenario 3: My Dream Job

  • Talk with a partner about your dream job
  • Say what skills you need and what qualifications you need
  • Take notes — you will present your partner's dream job

You: What is your dream job?

Partner: I want to be a teacher.

You: Why do you want this job?

Partner: Because I like working with children.

You: What skills do you need for it?

Partner: I need to be patient, and I need good communication skills.

You: Do you need any special qualifications or training?

Partner: Yes, I need a degree in education and some training in a school.

Quick Cheat Sheet

✅ can / can't

Subject + can + base verb
"I can use Excel."
"She can't drive."

⏪ could / couldn't

Past ability
"He could read before he was five."
"I couldn't swim until 2020."

🔄 be able to

am/is/are + able to + verb

🚨 Big Mistake

Never "cans", never "can to".
